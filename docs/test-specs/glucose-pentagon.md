# Comprehensive Glucose Pentagon

The Distribution report includes a Comprehensive Glucose Pentagon (CGP) and prognostic glycemic risk (PGR) for the selected calendar days. It compares five measures with the reference pentagon described by [Vigersky et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1932296817718561). The chart and values are descriptive research measures; the PGR is not a validated prediction of an individual's complications or a basis for treatment decisions.

The pentagon uses the model's fixed 70–180 mg/dL (approximately 3.9–10.0 mmol/L) range, regardless of the range selected for the surrounding Distribution report. Select all 24 hours to display it. The report shows CGM coverage for elapsed time, number of readings and selected days. For the current day, future hours are excluded from coverage; a partial day still yields estimates normalized to 24 hours. It withholds the pentagon when coverage is below 70% or the observed duration is less than 16 hours 48 minutes (70% of a 24-hour day). Coverage and observed duration are separate requirements: for example, a partial day can have 100% elapsed coverage but still have insufficient observations for a daily estimate. These are conservative implementation guards, not demonstrated validation thresholds for PGR; passing them does not establish that the selected data are representative.

## Calculation

The pentagon uses original CGM observations, before the report loader’s one-minute filter, and restricts them to 39–600 mg/dL. It collapses identical readings from the same device at the exact same timestamp; conflicting values at the same timestamp withhold the score. Simultaneous matching observations from different devices remain available for source-aware coverage. Observations are weighted by their estimated duration, at most five minutes. Coverage is calculated separately for each available device identity, using at most five minutes and the shorter neighboring interval, and overlapping intervals are counted only once. An interval crossing midnight is apportioned to the selected profile-local days. If readings from an indistinguishable source repeatedly alternate between shorter and longer gaps, including intervals that together approximate five minutes, the report withholds the score because overlapping uploads cannot be reliably distinguished from missing samples. This conservative pattern check tolerates small timing jitter and can flag a local run even in a long report; it is a heuristic, not a guarantee of identifying every ambiguous stream. Long gaps and completely missing days remain unobserved; calendar-day duration accounts for daylight saving. Time out of range is the observed fraction below 70 or above 180, expressed as minutes per 24 hours. The mean and population coefficient of variation are weighted by observed duration.

For each day, low intensity is the Euclidean length of the area below 70 (the glucose deficit multiplied by time) and the duration at or below 70. High intensity is the corresponding length of the area of glucose values at or above 180 and the duration at or above 180. Exactly 70 and 180 are included in the intensity durations, as in Reporter, while both are in range for time out of range. Each day's intensity is normalized to 24 hours of observed time, and daily intensities are averaged by observed duration. This extrapolates the observed pattern to a 24-hour day; missing time is not reconstructed and may bias the estimate. For example, 1,010 minutes all below range can yield an estimated 1,440 minutes/day out of range even though the rest of that day is unobserved. It may differ from Reporter when readings are missing or irregular, and daylight-saving days are normalized to 24 hours. Reporter labels intensity values in glucose-unit × min² and divides them by 1,000 for display.

The five calculated values are transformed to pentagon axes using the Vigersky/Reporter formulas; the PGR is the polygon area divided by the reference polygon area. The axis transforms use 17% as the lower bound for variability and 90 mg/dL as the lower bound for mean glucose, following Reporter. The displayed polygon is clipped at the chart's outer scale; dots identify clipped axes, but the displayed values and PGR use the full calculated values. No risk categories are assigned.

## Localization and layout

All report labels and warning states are registered in `translations/en/en.json`, the source configured in `crowdin.yml`. The limitations are separate complete sentences so translators can review and reuse them. Translations for individual languages follow the normal Crowdin review workflow; absent translations retain the existing English fallback. Adding the source strings does not mean those language translations have been completed.

Axis labels use wrapping HTML around the SVG, with automatic text direction, while the axis positions remain fixed. Numerical values retain left-to-right unit ordering. The reference outline is dashed and the selected-data outline is solid, so the legend can be understood without colour. Browser regressions cover expanded accented/emoji text, unspaced CJK text and right-to-left text at 320, 390 and 1440 pixels in both report themes. Print-media checks preserve the chart, values, limitations and distinct outline styles; they do not test every printer's pagination.

## Fixed numerical reference

`tests/fixtures/pentagon-reference.json` contains two varied days represented as duration/glucose bins, plus a case omitting the final four hours. Expected values were calculated separately with Python Decimal at 60-digit precision, without importing this implementation or using its output. Tests expand the bins into one- and five-minute samples in reverse order and compare all five metrics and the PGR against the fixed numbers.

For the complete case, observed duration is 2,880 minutes, the weighted glucose sum is 445,500 and the weighted squared-glucose sum is 83,989,500. Out-of-range duration is 1,260 minutes, giving 630 minutes/day and mean glucose 154.6875 mg/dL. Population variance is the weighted squared sum divided by duration minus the square of the mean. Each day's low/high intensity is calculated from its duration bins; after daily normalization and observed-duration weighting, the result is 1,440 times the sum of the daily unnormalized intensities divided by total observed duration. The area ratio simplifies to the sum of adjacent transformed-axis products divided by `5 × 14²`, because the common sine term cancels. This gives PGR **4.794097269391343**, or **3.89035671512699** when the final four hours are unobserved. Intermediate sums are retained in the fixture for review.

These are independently implemented numerical regression checks, not third-party clinical review or validation of PGR or the incomplete-data policy.
